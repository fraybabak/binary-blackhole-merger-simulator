What am I looking at?

Two black holes inspiral as they emit gravitational waves, losing orbital energy to the point of coalescence. Real astrophysical binaries take millions of years, but once inside the LIGO band the final plunge lasts only fractions of a second — this run stretches it to a watchable timescale. The ringed glow is the accretion disk, the black shadows are the event horizons, and the light arcs are real gravitational lensing of the background star field.

The default 36 + 29 M☉ binary reproduces GW150914 — the first gravitational-wave detection: 28.1 M☉ chirp mass, ≈3 M☉c² radiated, a 62 M☉ remnant spinning at χf ≈ 0.68.
